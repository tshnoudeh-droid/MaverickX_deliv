## The mining side: where MaverickX fits

Germanium is never mined on its own. It's scraped out of zinc processing with harsh chemistry, and less than 3% of what's in zinc ores and fly ash is ever recovered (Penn State). MaverickX's opening is the step that decides the cost: pulling tiny amounts of germanium out of a dilute liquid.

**How germanium is recovered today:**

1. Zinc ore is mined and milled. Germanium tags along as a trace, about 21 g/t in Titan's plant feed (SMM).
2. Residue is leached with strong mineral acids to get germanium into a liquid (Penn State).
3. Germanium is captured from that dilute liquid. The standard method, tannin precipitation, needs at least 8–10 g of tannin per g of germanium, sometimes over 300 g (MDPI). The alternative, hydrogen sulfide, is hazardous (CDC/USBM).
4. The concentrate is chlorinated and distilled in strong hydrochloric acid into germanium tetrachloride, then refined. For low-grade concentrates (under 1% germanium), acid and lime use climbs and wastewater can fail emission standards (patent).

MaverickX doesn't replace Teck's refinery; it upgrades Titan's dilute liquid into a concentrate Trail can take.

The science already points this way. A Helmholtz research team built "GaLIophore," which uses the siderophore DFOB to selectively capture gallium and germanium from industrial wastewater, forming highly stable complexes (GeoSaxonia 2024). An EU project found siderophores bind gallium, indium and germanium selectively even at very low concentrations (ERA-LEARN). Academics have the chemistry; MaverickX has what they lack: a team that produces siderophores at scale.

Honest comparison with tannin: by mass, siderophore use (8–19 g per g germanium, single use) is similar to tannin (8–10 g). Tannin is a cheap commodity, so MaverickX has to win on selectivity (a cleaner concentrate means less acid downstream) or reuse, not on price per kg.

## The semiconductor side: why AI needs these metals

AI's bottleneck is moving data and power between chips, and both fixes run on metals China controls: germanium for light (fiber and photonics) and gallium for power (GaN chips). NVIDIA, AMD and the AI labs aren't MaverickX's customers; they're the demand that makes the germanium worth recovering.

Two germanium paths feed AI today; the gallium path is a later extension of the same tool.

| Metal | Where it goes in AI hardware | Who's building with it | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Germanium | Core of optical fiber, raising its refractive index | Corning, under Meta's up to $6B fiber deal through 2030 (DCD) | Fiber is the largest germanium use: 142 t in 2026, peaking at 153 t in 2030 (S&P Global) |
| Germanium | Photodetectors in silicon photonics and co-packaged optics | NVIDIA Quantum-X and Spectrum-X Photonics switches, with TSMC and Corning in its ecosystem (Optica) | Silicon photonics platforms use germanium photodetectors (Research and Markets); NVIDIA calls co-packaged optics a structural requirement for future AI data centers (3DTested) |
| Gallium | GaN power transistors in 800V DC server racks | NVIDIA's 800V HVDC program with Navitas, Innoscience, Power Integrations and ST (Navitas; ST) | China controls ~98% of gallium production (Skillings) |

**Demand signals:**

- In the US, the biggest germanium uses are, in order: fiber optics, infrared optics, then semiconductors and solar cells (USGS).
- European germanium prices kept rising in 2026 on reduced China exports, AI infrastructure demand and defense buying (MMTA/Fastmarkets).
- Counterpoint to name on the site: high prices are already pushing some buyers to substitutes, mainly in infrared optics and solder (MMTA/Fastmarkets). Fiber and photonics are the stickier demand, which is why we lead with AI.
- The jump came right after China's controls, which is the case for a North American source: each kg recovered is worth about 5x what it was in 2021.

Gallium is phase 2, with the same tool. Siderophores were first designed to grab iron, and gallium mimics iron chemically, which is why the GaLIophore research targets gallium and germanium together. Teck's Trail expansion adds new gallium capacity, so the same Titan–Teck relationship could later cover gallium. Ask Suman before pitching it.

## How the website works

It's one scrolling page. As Suman scrolls, a full-screen video "plays" frame by frame, controlled by his scroll, while short text and numbers fade in over each scene. This is the Apple product-page technique, usually called scrollytelling.

**How it's built:**

1. Make ~6 short clips (5–8 seconds each), one per scene. AI video tools work, or simple 3D/motion renders.
2. Cut each clip into image frames with ffmpeg (about 60–90 frames per scene, WebP, 1280px wide).
3. Draw frames on a sticky `<canvas>`. Scroll position picks which frame shows. GSAP ScrollTrigger handles this cleanly.
4. Overlay text per scene: a headline, one or two numbers that count up, a source link.
5. Load scene by scene so the page opens fast; only the next scene's frames download ahead.

Why frames, not a normal video: scrubbing a `<video>` by scroll is choppy, especially on Safari and phones. An image sequence is smooth.

**Rules so it reads clearly:**

- One idea per scene, at most 2 numbers on screen at once
- Every number links to its source in a small footnote
- Estimates labelled "our estimate"
- Works without the animation: a "skip to summary" link at the top, and phones or reduced-motion settings get static key frames instead
- Total weight under ~20 MB, so it loads on any connection

How Suman gets it: by email (his stated preference), with the link plus a 3-line summary in the email body, so the pitch lands even if he never clicks.

## The 3-step offer and economics

The offer is low-risk because each step pays before the next. But at full scale, siderophore reuse decides whether this makes money, so the site should pitch the test as the way to find out, not claim it already works.

**The offer:**

- Now: a paid 90-day lab test on Titan's waste samples, estimated $25–75k.
- 2027–28: a paid on-site pilot, cost shared by Titan and government grants.
- 2029+: siderophore supply plus a 5–10% royalty: ~$3.9–7.8M/yr at $6,000/kg on 13,000 kg (our estimate).

**Siderophore cost per kg of germanium recovered** (our estimate; assumes $300/kg siderophore, 1:1 binding, 100% capture, so real numbers will be worse):

| Case | Siderophore needed per kg Ge | Cost per kg Ge | Margin at $6,000/kg (Western) | Margin at ~$3,800/kg (China) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small siderophore, single use | ~8 kg | ~$2,400 | ~$3,600 | ~$1,400 |
| Pyoverdine, single use | ~19 kg | ~$5,800 | ~$200 | loses ~$2,000 |
| Small siderophore, reused 10× | ~0.8 kg | ~$240 | ~$5,760 | ~$3,560 |
| Pyoverdine, reused 10× | ~1.9 kg | ~$580 | ~$5,420 | ~$3,220 |

The China price is approximate (27,250 yuan/kg on Oct 9, 2026, converted at ~7.1 yuan per dollar; SMM).

The takeaway: reused, it works at any price. Single-use pyoverdine only breaks even at Western prices. Suman said reuse is "in the works," so the 90-day test is exactly how to find out.

MaverickX's own cost per phase (team estimates, not verified): 90-day test ~$30–80k, roughly covered by the test fee; pilot ~$0.5–2M, mostly covered by Titan and grants; full scale needs siderophore production at volume, best done through a contract manufacturer so MaverickX doesn't build a plant.
